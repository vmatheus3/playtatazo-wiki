Autumn Woods

Levels 15–35

Golden and russet woods on rolling hills, full of acorns, antlers and bramble.

What it is like

WhereA continent about 1.2 km across, across the sea; its middle is about 2.2 km from the village.
DangerCreature levels 15–35, climbing from the harbour side (the coast facing the village) to the far side. More at night, on glacier peaks and in volcano craters.
TownsSafe towns with stations and a trading post kept by Hazel the Hedgehog
Looks likeRolling woods in red, copper and gold. Red maples and copper beeches grow together in groves, tall golden aspens and white paper birches stand in their own stands and by the water, and great old oaks spread their branches over the deepest woods. Between the trees are open glades. Blackberry brambles grow thickest at the edges of the woods, with red dogwood shrubs, fern patches turning rust and gold, heaps of fallen leaves with an acorn or two in them, and brown mushrooms on mossy log ends. Grey boulders spotted with orange and green lichen sit in the glades, and hollow fallen logs lie on the forest floor. Pumpkins still grow on their vines in the clearings. Now and then, out in a glade, a tall granite tor stands split down the middle, a small red tree growing out of the crack: you can see it from far away.
WeatherFalling leaves in golden light. Felled trees shed orange leaves.
MusicA tin whistle and strummed strings, with rustling leaves and songbirds.
Region namesAmberleaf Woods, Russet Hills, Maplefire Glade, Goldenbough Vale…

Watch out

  • The Hollow Scarecrow stands still all day and walks at night.
  • Bramble Bears hit hard with a ground pound: step out of the ring.
  • The Old Hollow Oak wanders the deepest woods.
  • Thornwood the Elder, the oldest oak of all, walks the far side of the woods.
