Getting around, and animal friends

First steps

Roads, gliders, canoes, the Sky Rower, ferries, ziplines, fast travel, capybara friends and horses.

Roads

Roads join the village to every town, each land's harbour and a few of its great landmarks. Follow one and you won't get lost: every road leads somewhere worth going. Every land has them, and every world's roads are its own.

  • What they're made of: cobbles near towns and the village, lit by lamps on the last stretch in. Out in the country they turn into a dirt track (hard-packed in the desert), a gravel track through the Frostlands' snow and the Ember Wastes' cinders, and a raised gravel causeway across wetlands. On the Primeval Isle it's only a trail through the ferns.
  • Rivers: where a road meets a narrow river there is a bridge: a stone arch near towns, a wooden footbridge or a rope bridge in the wilds. A wide river has a ford, shallow enough to wade.
  • Forks: a signpost stands wherever roads meet. Milestones mark the way out in the country.
  • Along the way: farms with fields, fences and scarecrows, wells and water troughs, carts and wagons resting by the road, benches and picnic tables, travellers' camps, and each land's own things (fruit stands, apple carts, firewood racks, water jars, mine carts, firefly lanterns). On a hilltop with a view you'll often find a bench to sit and look out.
  • On the map (M) roads are the pale lines between towns.
  • Quicker on the road: on a road or a bridge you walk, run and ride 15% faster. It works on a horse or a capybara too, but not wading a ford. Nothing grows on a road and trees stand back from it, so a road is the easy way to cross a land.
  • Tip: going somewhere far? Head for the nearest road first. The road is the quick way there, even if it bends.

Leaf Glider

Craft a Leaf Glider at a workbench and carry it. Jump, then press and hold jump again in the air: the leaf opens and you float down gently, drifting forward. It uses a little stamina; an empty stamina bar folds the leaf.

Canoe

Hold a Canoe and use it on water at least a quarter of a metre deep to launch it. E gets in and out. Paddle with the move keys; river currents carry you along.

You can paddle a canoe across the sea to the other lands (or swim, if you don't mind the long way). Near the world's edge the sea gets too wild, and a canoe won't go any further. See Continents and danger.

Sky Rower

The Sky Rower is a little plank boat with two cloth wings, and it flies when you row. Rill the Otter at the pier dreams it up with you from level 5 in his story "The Sky Rower": bring him feathers, build it at a Workbench (6 planks, 4 bamboo, 3 cloth, 6 feathers, 2 rope and 1 resin), then fly.

  • Set it down: hold it and use it on open ground or water. E climbs in. E again steps out, once you have landed. Crouch and E on an empty one packs it back up.
  • Row: each press of Space (A on a gamepad) is one oar stroke. The wings beat, the boat rises a little and speeds up. A stroke costs a bit of stamina: a full bar pays for about 16 strokes, and they come at most every 0.35 s.
  • Steer: forward keeps up a cruising speed of 6 m/s, back slows you down, left and right turn. Strokes push you up to 11 m/s.
  • Glide: stop rowing and it glides, sinking slowly (1.6 m/s). Your stamina doesn't come back in the air, except a little while you glide without rowing.
  • Land: it lands wherever it touches the ground, and on water it floats like a boat.
  • Not too high: more than 40 m above the ground, the air is too thin and strokes stop lifting.

To fly far, row up high first, then glide, and row again in short bursts when you get low. After each flight you hear how far you went.

Your proud moment. When you finish one of Rill's quests, the game gives you a moment to take in what you made. For about half a minute the controls rest and the Sky Rower flies a slow lap on its own, then glides down. The camera watches from outside, like a film: from far away, from the ground, round the boat, close on Tatazo's proud face, and last Rill walks over to stand beside you and says a few words from the heart. You are safe the whole time, and the pause menu still works. Then the lesson card shows and you carry on right where you are. It plays once, and your journal's Notes page keeps a Watch again button for it.

Ferries

Every land has a harbour on its side facing the village: a wooden pier with a ferry moored at the end (the village's own harbour faces the Dragonfire Isle). Walk out along the pier and press E at the ferry. It lists the other lands with their creature levels, how far they are and the fare, a few orange coins (longer trips cost more). Pick one and you sail across in a few seconds, stepping off onto the other land's pier.

Ziplines

Place two Zipline Posts within 80 m of each other and a cable links them. E at a post rides to the other one; jump lets go.

Fast travel and pins

  • The village, your bed, the towns you've visited, and the lookouts and temples you've found are travel points. Open the map (M), click one and confirm. You can't travel within 10 seconds of being hurt.
  • Pins: click the map to drop a pin (up to 12). The compass shows them.

Capybara friends

Wild capybaras graze by rivers and lakes in jungle, wetland and meadow. To make a friend, hold carrots, corn, grain, rice, roots, pumpkins or a mango and press E on one three times. A friend follows you; crouch and E asks it to wait. You can have up to three. With a Leaf Saddle in your satchel, press E to saddle it and E again to ride. Furs come in brown, golden, dark, a rare cream and a very rare mango.

Horses

Dottie the Donkey keeps a ranch a short walk outside the village. Help her with her errand (hay, apples or carrots, and rope) and she teaches you how to befriend horses gently, and the recipes for a Rope Bridle and a Horse Whistle.

To befriend a wild horse: tire it out gently until it's below about a third of its strength, then hold a bridle, press E and hold still. A tamed horse gets a name and lives in your stable. Ride it with E; sprint to gallop. The whistle calls it from far away.

The Moonwing Pegasus

On some clear nights, a white winged horse lands on a high peak and leaves at dawn. Only a Starlight Bridle can calm it, and whoever does is announced to the whole world. Its rider can fly.
