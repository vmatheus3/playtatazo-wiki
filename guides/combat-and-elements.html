Combat and elements

Fighting and gear

How hits land, what elements do, and how to read a creature's attack.

Calming, not hurting

When you "fight" in TATAZO you are cleansing the Gloom. A cleansed Gloom creature bursts into clean water and leaves. A gloom-touched animal shakes off the murk and trots home. You get XP, orange coins and whatever it leaves behind.

How hard you hit

Every hit you land is somewhere between 40% and 100% of your biggest possible hit. The biggest hit grows with:

  • your weapon's attack,
  • your skill with that kind of weapon (melee, distance or magic), which rises every time you use it,
  • your level,
  • and your class: each class is a little better with its own style.

Your Satchel shows it: Attack is the biggest normal hit with what is in your hand, and Magic the biggest hit from a magic skill. Both go up with every level.

Your skills keep up with your level. Skills grow as you use them, but if you've levelled up mostly by gathering, jobs and exploring, a level-up lifts your class's fighting skills to at least 70% of the way to what a fighter of your level has. Training still takes you further.

Sometimes a hit is critical and lands ×1.6 as hard. Everyone starts with a 5% chance; Ninjas get more, and some gear adds more (see Item powers).

Everyone also has the Tail Spin (Q): a spin that hits everything around you. It grows with your level and melee skill.

Your class's skills sit on the 1–8 bar, next to your food and potions: their number keys cast them. Each new skill goes onto a free slot as you learn it, and the Skill Book (K) lets you drag them where you like. R, F, T and G are optional extra keys for any four skills (a controller's d-pad casts those four). No class yet? See Picking your class for the five paths and how to try one before you commit.

The weapon you fight with is the one you wear in the Hand slot of your gear. Every attack swings it, however you attack (a click, the use button, auto-attack): Tatazo draws it himself and puts away the tool or food he was holding. A weapon in your satchel never fights, so wear the one you want. A weapon worn out, or too high a level for you, is passed over and he says why. With no weapon worn he bites: he rears back and snaps his jaws shut. At first a bite is almost as strong as a first crafted sword, and it grows as you level up, a little more slowly than a weapon would.

The elements

Hits carry an element. Most are plain physical; magic staves, some weapons and some abilities carry another:

ElementWhere it comes from
FireSunstone staves, fire gear, the Ember Wastes' creatures
IceRiver-water staves, frost creatures
EnergyStorm staves and wands, storm eels, sparks
EarthMushroom and leaf creatures, and some dragons
HolyClean-water bolts, the Sunblade and the old guardians' blades
DecayA few creatures of the night

Every creature takes more damage from its weaknesses and less from what it resists. A creature page shows it like this: "Fire +45%" means fire hits it 45% harder; "Ice −60%" means ice barely tickles it. As a rule:

  • Gloom creatures hate holy clean water, and many hate fire.
  • Frost creatures hate fire; fire creatures hate ice.
  • Water creatures hate earth; the ones that throw sparks shrug off energy.
  • Golems shrug off earth; armoured beasts are easier with magic and lightning than with blades.

Reading an attack

Every creature winds up before it strikes, and you can see it coming. Each kind of attack has a way to dodge it:

AttackWhat you seeWhat to do
BiteIt crouchesBack away, then strike while it recovers
Rolling ramIt starts to spinSidestep, then hit it while it is dizzy
ChargeIt lowers its headSidestep, then hit back while it catches its breath
SwoopIt rises into the airKeep moving: it dives at empty ground
LeapA ring on the groundStep out of the ring
Ground poundIt raises its armsStep back out of the ring
SparksIt glowsStep sideways; the sparks fly past
Claw sweepA wide swing (or hissing steam)Stay behind it; back away when the steam starts

Every creature page has a tip written the same way the game shows it when you are knocked out.

Stronger creatures

  • Where: at home, creatures get stronger the further you are from the village; every other land has its own band of levels. See Continents and danger.
  • Night: everything is a bit stronger after dark.
  • Elites: from level 6, about 5% of creatures are elites: bigger, golden-eyed, with ×2.2 health and ×1.4 attack. They give ×3 XP and better loot (the odds are in Lucky drops and rarity).
  • Mini-bosses and bosses: see Bosses.
Too easy?

If a creature is more than 10 levels below you, it gives less XP (a little less for each level, down to a tenth). Head further out: the XP there is much better.

Beyond level 130

No creature is ever above level 130, but you can keep going. Every level past it adds 5% to every hit and spell you land on a creature:

Your levelYour hits on creatures
130×1
150×2
200×4.5
300×9.5
500×19.5
999×44.45

So around level 150 the strongest creatures are a comfortable fight, and from about 300 most of them fall in one or two hits. Up to level 130 nothing changes: the climb stays a real adventure. It counts against creatures and bosses (and on the training dummies, so you can see it), never in duels between players. The whole climb is in Levels and power.

Armour and shields

Armour soaks up part of every physical hit. A shield in your off hand blocks part of any hit that comes from the front. Some gear also resists an element (for example "resists fire 20%").

Tips

  • Fight with friends. A party shares XP and everyone gets their own loot (see Level sync and parties).
  • Watch the bottom right after a fight. Each thing you pick up shows there as a faint line, like "+3 Feather". The same thing picked up again adds to its line. Up to 5 lines show, and each fades after 4 seconds. Only your own pickups show. You can turn them off in Settings, on the GAME tab: Pickup notices.
  • Carry food and a few potions. Health only grows back while you are fed. Never want to run dry? See Hero's Health and Mana Potions.
  • Need a break from creatures? Use the Calm Aura.
