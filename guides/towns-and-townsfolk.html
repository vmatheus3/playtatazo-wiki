Towns and townsfolk

More guides

The towns in every land, what's in them, the people who live there and how to talk to them.

Towns everywhere

Past the village, many regions have a town at their heart. Each one is safe ground: no creature spawns or attacks there, and nobody digs or builds inside it. Walking in for the first time counts as a discovery, and the town becomes a travel point on your map.

Every town is built for its land:

  • Riverlands: river towns with timber houses and flower boxes. Towns on a slope get stone walls and a lookout.
  • Autumn Woods: forest villages with cabins, orchards and a cider barn.
  • Frostlands: snowy lodge towns with firewood racks, dog sleds and ice-fishing shacks.
  • Sunscorch Desert: oases with adobe houses, caravan tents and water jars.
  • Ember Wastes: forge towns of stone houses around a hot-spring bathhouse.
  • Glowcap Hollow: mushroom villages with cap cottages and lantern market huts.
  • Zombie Land: lantern-lit refuges for travellers in the mist.
  • The Primeval Isle: one explorers' outpost (see below).
  • The Dragonfire Isle: one dragon keepers' town (see below).

A town close to the water also gets a harbour with a pier, boats, fishing nets and a fish stall.

No two towns are laid out the same, but you'll always find your way.

The island towns

You step off the ferry onto the Primeval Isle. Something enormous stomps in the ferns, and up the trail you spot canvas roofs and a lantern: the explorers' outpost. On the Dragonfire Isle, the last climb to the Dragon Tower starts from the dragon keepers' town, where red-and-gold banners snap in the hot wind.

Each isle has just one town. It stands between the middle of the isle, where the naturalist's camp and the Dragon Tower are, and the coast. Most often it's on the harbour side, and a road from the pier leads to its gates. Like every town it's safe ground, with a keeper's trading post, a locker and the workbench, anvil, furnace, campfire and cooking pot. Each isle has its own keeper: Iggy the Iguana, an explorer who sketches every plant and sells field supplies, and Bram the Badger, a dragon keeper who sells hearth goods and chili for cold tower nights. Befriend them for their treat recipes.

  • The explorers' outpost (Primeval Isle): research huts on stilts, canvas tents, plant presses drying giant fern leaves, supply crates and a fossil dig at the edge of town. It always has a Fossil Museum, so bring the fossils you dig up on the island straight to its curator. Explorers map the trails and tiptoe past sleeping dinosaurs, and a naturalist's apprentice presses one leaf from every plant.
  • The dragon keepers' town (Dragonfire Isle): banners round the plaza and a hearth fire at its heart, long timber-and-stone halls with bronze roofs, and a tall dragon perch at the edge of town where a friendly dragon can land. The dragon keepers care for the dragons and sing to calm them at dusk, and the saddler stitches every strap twice.

Tip: the isles are the hardest lands in the game. Walk into the island town first. It becomes a travel point, so after a tough fight you can travel back to safe ground and try again (see Getting around, and animal friends).

Finding your way around

Four main streets cross at the plaza and run out to the town's four gates, where the roads begin. The plaza has the town's fountain or well, benches and the notice board. Around it you'll find:

  • The market: the keeper's trading post, fruit, fish and cloth stalls, the general store and the bakery.
  • The crafts corner: a workbench, an anvil and a furnace anyone can use, the blacksmith's forge and the pottery kiln.
  • The tea garden: the tea house, picnic tables, and a campfire and cooking pot for everyone.
  • The fun corner: the music stage, the puppet theatre and the swings.
  • Homes along the streets, with a library, a town hall or a Fossil Museum among them (about half the towns have a museum), and farms, gardens, chicken coops, windmills or stables at the edges.

Town sites are checked for dry gates, including the island towns. Roads meet those gates and avoid the sea; roadside lamps stand on dry ground. Required landmarks such as a town's Fossil Museum keep a place in its layout. A harbour on separate land may lead to a nearby landmark instead of a town. Follow the local signs rather than expecting every coast to connect directly.

Press E on the plaza's notice board to read the stories of the land: the storylines told at its named places, which way each one starts from town, how far along you are, and a pin on your map for the nearest one you haven't finished (see Things to discover).

The townsfolk

Every town has its own people: bakers, fruit sellers, smiths, potters, tea-keepers, musicians, puppeteers, gardeners, farmers, fishers, sweepers, lamplighters, museum curators and more, plus children who love the swings and the puppet show. The isles add their own folk: explorers and a naturalist's apprentice at the outpost, dragon keepers and a saddler in the keepers' town. Each one has a name and something special about them. One baker hums to the bread, and one fisher always throws the little fish back.

Every town has its animals too. Hens peck about the coop, a friendly dog trots round the plaza and a ginger cat naps by a doorstep. At night the hens are tucked up in the coop.

They keep a loose day:

  • Morning: they leave home after sunrise and go to work. The baker opens the bakery, stall-holders stand by their stalls and the smith hammers at the anvil.
  • Afternoon: they do what they love. Some fish off the pier, some sip tea, and some gather at the music stage or the puppet theatre.
  • Evening: they walk home along the streets at dusk. At night the town sleeps, and the lamps stay lit.

Everyone in a town sees the same people in the same places, so you can meet friends at the bakery in the morning.

Fair days

Every few days a town holds a fair. In the afternoon, nearly everyone gathers in front of the music stage: the musician plays, the puppeteer puts on a show and the children play nearby. When you walk into a town on its fair day, you'll hear about it.

Talking to townsfolk

Walk up to someone and they'll often wave hello. Their names show when you're close: the person you aim at first, then the nearest few, so a crowd stays readable. To chat, aim at them and press E ("Talk"). They'll tell you about themselves and their work, or share a little lesson about kindness, honesty or caring for the world.

  • A museum curator puts every fossil you've found on the museum's shelves.
  • Now and then someone has lost a pet this week and will ask you to look for it. When you find it, come back and tell them.

Shopping at the stalls

The smell of fresh bread drifts across the plaza, and the baker waves you over. The townsfolk who keep a trade sell their own goods. Talk to one and their stall opens right there:

  • Baker: bread, loaves and pies, and a jar of honey for the rolls.
  • Fruit seller: the land's own fruit. Mangoes and pineapples in the Riverlands, dates and melons in the desert, frost berries in the snow.
  • Fishmonger: the day's catch and a little bait.
  • Smith: starter tools and plain, sturdy gear.
  • Cook: warm stews and soups.
  • Farmer: seeds and farm tools. Gardener: saplings, flower seeds and plant pots.
  • Beekeeper: honey and flower seeds. Potter: pots, clay and bricks. Tea-keeper: blossom tea.
  • Storekeeper: rope, planks, torches, simple potions and ammo.

Stalls take only orange coins. Prices are the same as the keepers' shops: about twice what a thing is worth. Each stall also buys back goods of its own kind, at about half their worth, up to a daily amount. Everything restocks every morning at 6:00. Rare finds are never sold at a stall. Take those to the market board (see The market and trading).

Tip: heading out on a long walk? Stop by the cook and the baker first. A full satchel of food keeps you going between towns.

The town's keeper still runs the trading post in the market (see Villagers and town keepers) and opens your locker (see Storage: satchel, chests, the locker and the vault).
