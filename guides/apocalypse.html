"Apocalypse: how long can you last?"

More guides

A night that never ends, three times the creatures, one life, and weekly prizes for the best on the board.

The lanterns go out. Something rustles in the reeds, then something else, then a dozen more. It's night, and it will stay night. Your gator plants its feet. How long can you last — and where will your name sit on the board when the week ends?

Apocalypse is a quick, fierce run with one of your gators, not a world you keep. Nothing from a run changes your gator's items, levels or coins: no items lost, no coins spent, no levels earned or dropped. What you keep afterwards is your score, your badges, your title, your place on the board next to every player who ran that week — and, if you're well placed, a prize.

What makes a run

Pick APOCALYPSE under FAMILY in the gator lobby:

  1. Pick one of your gators. It comes exactly as it is: level, class, skills, gear and satchel.
  2. Choose SOLO or WITH FRIENDS, and press START.
  3. Friends can join through MULTIPLAYER in the run's first 90 seconds. After that the board is locked: no joining a run already under way, so nobody's board is unfair.

A run happens in its own throwaway copy of the world. It's wiped the moment it ends, and every game night the world resets to a fresh one anyway, so no build ever survives there.

The rules of the night

Everything from your usual Adventure run is turned up, and a few things are turned off:

  • It's always night. The sun never rises in a run.
  • Creatures come 3 times as thick around you, and faster, from the first minute.
  • The night deepens. Every 4 minutes it grows a step darker: the creature caps climb (up to 6 times the usual night), new creatures come 2 levels tougher, and more of them are elites. Every run ends sooner or later — nobody outlasts the night forever.
  • Potions never run out. Drink as many as you like, as often as you like.
  • No Calm Aura, and no safe ground. Even the village and the towns won't keep creatures off you.
  • One life. Fall once, and your run is over on the spot.
  • Run-only finds. Creatures still drop gear, food and potions at the world's usual rate, and you can use every bit of it during the run. It all stays in the run and vanishes when it ends. No XP and no coins: your level stays exactly what it was, so the band you're ranked in never moves under you mid-run.

Your score

The end screen adds it up line by line, in the open — no hidden bonuses, nothing you can't see:

What you didPoints
Each creature calmed1
Each elite5
Each mini-boss25
Every 10 seconds you last1

Every one of those numbers is counted by the world's own server, from what actually happened in the fight — never from anything a player's game merely says happened. A run where the rules were changed or host commands were used posts nothing at all: see "Fair play" below.

The boards: everyone who ran, and your friends

Every week brings a new map and new boards, the same map for everyone that week (so friends fight the same ground), plus an all-time board that keeps the best run ever. There are three kinds of board:

BoardWhat it ranks
Scorethe score table above
Longest nightseconds survived
Most creatures calmedcreatures calmed

Scores are only ever compared fairly, level band by level band, so a level-20 gator is never judged next to a level-200 one:

BandLevels
11 to 49
250 to 99
3100 to 149

In a team, the highest level in the team picks the band. Solo runs and runs with friends have their own boards, so they never mix.

Two views of the same board:

  • Friends, right there on the APOCALYPSE screen and in the run's own panel: your friends' gator names (never their Steam names), where you stand among them, and how far to the next one ("12 more points to pass Ana").
  • Global, for the weekly prizes below: every player anywhere who recorded a score that week, not only your friends. That's the ranking the owner asked for — "the ranking should be global for every player that records a score."

Friends' scores and the global rank both need Steam. Without it, your own bests are still kept on your computer.

Your title grows with your longest night ever: Lantern Bearer at 10 minutes, Night Warden at 30, Dawn Seeker at 60, and Keeper of the Long Night at 120. You choose whether to show it on the APOCALYPSE screen.

Weekly prizes

Somewhere out there this week, a gator is about to finish top ten on the global score board — and walk away with a Double XP Potion, a Double Drop Charm, the Weekly Champion frame, and a pocketful of coins. When the week closes, every player who recorded a score on the global SCORE board is paid, by rank:

Where you landYou get
Top 10Double XP Potion (3 days) + Double Drop Charm (3 days) + the Weekly Champion frame + 2,000 coins
11 to 50Double XP Potion (1 day) + 800 coins + crafting materials for your band
51 to 100Double XP Potion (6 hours) + 300 coins
Everyone elseNothing this week — the board resets, and so does your chance

A Double XP Potion doubles every bit of XP you earn while it's running, on the gator that drinks it, in the world you drink it in. A Double Drop Charm rolls every creature's loot twice for as long as it lasts. Drinking a second one before the first runs out makes the time last longer, not the doubling stronger — one at a time is already generous. The Weekly Champion frame is yours to keep and show once you've ever placed in the top ten.

Prizes wait 24 hours after the week closes before they're handed out — a fairness check on that week's scores — then they're waiting for you on the APOCALYPSE screen in the lobby. Pick a gator and press COLLECT: the coins and items go straight to that gator's own market mailbox, ready to open next time you visit that world.

Why only the score board decides a prize?

The three boards measure different things, but a prize needs one ranking everyone agrees on. Score already blends how many you calmed, how tough they were, and how long you lasted — the fairest single number for a pack of prizes.

Fair play

Every number on a run's own board comes from the world's server watching the fight happen — never from a player's word. A few things follow from that, plainly:

  • Cheats and rule changes post nothing. A run where a host used dev commands, or changed the world's rules mid-run, is thrown out before it's ever sent to a board.
  • An impossible pace is refused. No real fight, at any level, can calm creatures faster than a run could ever need to; a report that fast is rejected outright.
  • A merely unusual pace is only flagged, quietly, for whoever hosts that world to notice — it still posts, since the counters are the server's own, not a guess.
  • One prize per player, per week, per board. Placing in more than one band or mode in the same week can still win more than one prize; placing twice in the same one can't.
  • What this can't promise: this game has no central server watching every player at once — each world's own copy of the game keeps its own counters. Steam's leaderboard takes any signed-in account's upload as it comes. That's honest, not a weakness hidden from you.

What you can do about it

  • Know your band before you start. Your level picks it; a level near the top of a band (49, 99, 149) fights the easiest creatures for the points on offer.
  • Keep your streak. Elites are worth 5 points each, but a long life adds up faster than one big fight. Calm the small ones first so they can't wear you down, and drink a potion before you need it — they never run out.
  • Bring a Double XP or Double Drop prize into a run you're not ranking. They work on any of that gator's worlds, any time — save them for a session where you're pushing levels or hunting gear, not necessarily your next Apocalypse run (loot found during a run never leaves it anyway).
  • Aim for the boards you can actually move. The all-time board is a long climb; the weekly one resets, so a fresh week is always a fresh chance at a prize.
Every week is a clean slate

Missed the top 100 this week? The board — and the map — reset next Monday. Your gator, its gear and its levels are exactly where you left them, so there's nothing to lose by trying again.
